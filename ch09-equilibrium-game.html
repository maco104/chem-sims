A reaction that runs both ways

Some reactions run in both directions at once. When carbon dioxide dissolves in water, some of it reacts to make carbonic acid, while some carbonic acid falls apart back into carbon dioxide and water:

CO₂(g) + H₂O(l) ⇌ H₂CO₃(aq)

We can’t watch molecules do this, but we can model it: two teams on opposite sides of a line throw snowballs across. Each team tries to keep its own side clear by throwing the snowballs on its side over the line.

  • a snowball = a molecule
  • water = the liquid all around, not a snowball
  • reactant side = CO2
  • product side = H2CO3
  • a throw across = the forward reaction (→)
  • a throw back = the reverse reaction (←)

How to play

  1. Read the situation, then pick your call (or type a number) and press Lock in my call. Most calls are made before the fight runs.
  2. Watch the run. Every 5 seconds of fighting is one round: at the end of each round both sides are counted, and the counts go into the round log and the graph.
  3. The scoreboard shows each side’s count and how fast it is throwing (throws/s).
  4. The feedback tells you what happened in your run and why.
  5. On a phone the field is turned: the reactant side is on top.

Four levels, about 25 minutes. Keep a calculator handy for Level 3. At the end you get a scouting report: your calls by skill, and what to review.
